Assignment #3

Anti-Self Portrait

A self-portrait usually says who you are. This one defines you by your anti-identity, by what you are not, in one photograph and one page that explains it.

Draft — not final. Do not build to this yet.

  • What am I most not?
  • What is my exact opposite in character, in taste, in what I believe, in how I look or live?
  • What am I against, and what would I look like if I became it?
  • Which of these can a photograph show, and by what means?

01The process
  1. 01Write down what you are. Then write the opposite of each line, and mark what you are most not.
  2. 02Ask two or three friends or classmates what you are really like. Their answers can show you an opposite you had not thought of.
  3. 03Choose the angle of your anti-self. It can be conceptual or philosophical, as an opposite character, taste or belief, the thing you are against, or the person you would become if you turned into it. It can also be photographic or technical, as the opposite of how you usually photograph.
  4. 04You are the director. Choose the place, bring the props and the wardrobe, and decide the light, the framing and the composition.
  5. 05Make the photograph. Try the idea in more than one visual way and compare each attempt with what you want to say.
  6. 06Choose one photograph, and write one A4 page about it.
02Limitations
! Less is more

Leave out knives, ketchup, masks and weapons. Say as much as you can as simply as you can, and what remains in the picture will say more.

Photographs
One.
Text
One A4 page about what you are not, in which respect the photograph is anti and against what, and how the picture shows it.
Mise en scène
Place, props, wardrobe, light, framing and composition, all chosen by you.
03What you will learn
  • +Defining a concept before photographing it
  • +Directing a photograph through place, props, wardrobe, light and framing
  • +Matching a visual language to an idea, and judging how closely the two meet
  • +Writing about your own photograph clearly and briefly
04Tips
  • ·Look at the photograph without the text. Ask what it says on its own.
  • ·An opposite can be shown by one object, one gesture or one change of light.
