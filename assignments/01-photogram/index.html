Assignment #1

Photogram

Five photograms made without a camera, showing a full range of tones rather than two.

01The process
  1. 01You can work individually or as a group. Working collectively is highly recommended.
  2. 02Prepare your materials. Gather objects that will make interesting shapes and patterns — consider different textures, sizes and levels of transparency.
  3. 03In the darkroom, arrange your objects on the photographic paper. Vary the placement across the five sheets.
  4. 04Expose the paper to light, managing the exposure time. Change it between sheets and watch what it does to contrast and detail.
  5. 05Process in developer, stop bath and fixer. Watch the development rather than timing it blind.
  6. 06Bring the five finished photograms to class on the hand-in date, dried and ready to show.
02Limitations
! Tones — not only two

Your photograms must show a range of tones and gradation, not just black and white. Experiment with exposure times and materials to get depth and contrast. Your work is expected to show that variety.

Exposure time
Adjust to the objects and the effect you are after.
Materials
Any object that fits on the paper — leaves, lace, feathers, glass, liquids.
Location
Darkroom.
Paper
Standard black-and-white photographic paper, 13 × 18 cm or larger.
Camera
None. Nothing in this assignment is made with a lens.
03What you will learn
  • +Darkroom technique, including dodging and burning
  • +Composing by placing objects and controlling exposure
  • +The darkroom process for photography made without a camera
04Tips
  • ·Test your exposure on one sheet before making the final five.
  • ·Make a simple plan for each composition before you start.
  • ·Try layering objects.
  • ·Try multiple exposures for different tones.
  • ·Place objects flat on the paper — a lifted edge exposes underneath and blurs.
  • ·Note your exposure time and placement as you go, so you can repeat what worked.
