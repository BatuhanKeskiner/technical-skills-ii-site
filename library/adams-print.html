Look up

the paper's exposure scalep.157
A negative with an opacity range of 1:25 has a density range of 1.40, and that is the log exposure range a paper needs to give a full scale. Our own curve runs 1.38 from toe to shoulder.
the paper's characteristic curvep.155-157
Toe = the high values, white because little silver is there. Shoulder = the low values, where much more silver has been laid down. The straight section's slope is the contrast.
paper gradesp.61-64
And the thing that is almost never said: manufacturers exclude the toe and the shoulder when they set a grade, so a grade figure describes the straight section only.
brilliance is not contrastp.17
Adams measured Frederick Evans's platinum prints and found their whole range of reflection densities was 1.20 - far less than he expected, and they read as astonishingly brilliant.
test stripsp.83-84, 94-95
reflection densityp.156
What a densitometer measures off a print, and the unit every number in our test strip is in.
reproduction rangesp.202
For reproduction prints Adams and George Waters settled on a 1.50 reflection-density range for the textural range, excluding the deepest blacks and the true whites.
